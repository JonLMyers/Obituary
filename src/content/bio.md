Robert lived his life with his hands, his heart, and a stubborn determination that made everything he touched a little better. For 30 years, he worked hard to give his family the life he believed they deserved. But when the workday ended, he stepped into the roles he loved most—husband, dad, craftsman, fisherman, storyteller.

He was a master of his crafts, earning awards in taxidermy and building just about anything he set his mind to. If he wasn’t working on his boat or tending to his saltwater tanks, he was out on the water chasing the next big catch. The Walleye Fall Brawl wasn’t just a tournament to him—he wanted that truck.

Robert filled his family’s life with traditions and adventures: holidays, vacations, walks, movies, fishing trips, and Euchre tournaments that he took far more seriously than anyone else. He joined his wife, Angela, on her wine tours, walked Snickers with pride, and never hesitated to jump into whatever wild project his daughters dreamed up.

He loved telling stories from his childhood: stories of farm life, state fairs, and the lakes and rivers that shaped him. He worked hard as a kid, and those early lessons stayed with him for life.

What his family will miss most is the way he guided them. He believed in doing things the right way, even when it was the hard way. He asked for their thoughts, offered his own wisdom freely, and taught them how to live with integrity, curiosity, love, and grit.

He is preceded in death by his father Lawrence John Myers and mother Sheila (nee Cebula). He is survived by his wife, Angela; his five children, Jonathan, Haley, Emily, Kaitlyn, and Megan; and his brothers Kenny and Keith.

We invite you to continue what he loved to do: share his stories. Please share your memories, pictures, and more.
