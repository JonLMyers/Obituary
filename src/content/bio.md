Robert John Myers, age 63, of Amherst, Ohio unexpectedly passed away on April 22, 2026. He was born in Elyria, to Sheila and Lawrence. Robert was a graduate of Keystone High School and College of ATI in Wooster, then moved to Thomasville, Georgia to pursue internships in agriculture and animal management. Robert came back to Ohio to build his career in construction.

Robert lived his life with his hands, his heart, and a stubborn determination that made everything he touched a little better. For 30 years, he worked hard to give his family the life he believed they deserved. But when the workday ended, he stepped into the roles he loved most—husband, dad, craftsman, fisherman, and storyteller.
He was a master of his crafts, earning awards in taxidermy and building just about anything he set his mind to. If he wasn’t working or tending to his saltwater tanks, he was out on the water chasing the next big catch. The Walleye Fall Brawl wasn’t just a tournament to him—he wanted that prize. 

Robert filled his family’s life with traditions and adventures: holidays, vacations, walks, movies, fishing trips, and Euchre tournaments that he took far more seriously than anyone else. He joined his wife, Angela, on her wine tours, park walks with Snickers, and never hesitated to jump into whatever wild projects his children dreamed up.
He loved telling stories from his childhood: stories of farm life, state fairs, and the lakes and rivers that shaped him. He worked hard as a kid, and those early lessons stayed with him for life.

His family will miss the way he guided them. He believed in doing things the right way, even when it was the hard way. He asked for their thoughts, offered his own wisdom freely, and taught them how to live with integrity, curiosity, love, and resilience.

He is preceded in death by his father Lawrence John Myers and mother Sheila (nee Cebula). He is survived by his wife, Angela; his five children, Jonathan, Haley, Emily, Kaitlyn, and Megan; his step-father Gary Painter; and his brothers Kenneth and Keith.  

We invite you to continue what he loved to do: share his stories. Please share your memories, pictures, and more.

In lieu of flowers, donations can be made to the [National Fish and Wildlife Foundation](https://www.nfwf.org)
